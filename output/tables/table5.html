Standardized prevalence: predicted prevalence from the sex x BMI interaction model averaged over the survey-weighted covariate distribution of the cohort. A negative difference (women minus men) means a larger absolute excess in men. RERI, AP and SI use normal-weight women, the lowest-risk group, as the reference, so the second exposure is male sex (Knol et al. 2011); RERI > 0, AP > 0 and SI > 1 indicate more than additive joint association of the BMI category and male sex. Odds ratios approximate risk ratios less well when an outcome is common (hypertension; multimorbidity at older ages). SI is not estimable when its denominator is not positive in some bootstrap replicates. Models adjusted for age group, household income, household education, smoking, alcohol and physical activity. Bootstrap variance (1,000 replicates) pooled over 20 imputations.
